The problem

Some bugs need more than one person.

You cannot test presence, read receipts, live sync, notification fan-out, or "does deleting this remove it for everyone" while being one human with one account. You end up juggling two phones and hoping.

And an app with nobody in it tells you nothing about how it feels to use. Empty feeds, empty maps, empty leaderboards — you ship a cold start you have never once seen warm.

Today

Two phones, three test accounts, a note of who's logged in where — and no idea whether your permission rules hold under concurrent writes.

With Populace

Fifty people using your app at once, each with a genuinely separate identity, and a report naming every endpoint that failed and exactly how slow it got.

How it works

One adapter. That's the entire integration.

The engine knows how to be a person. It knows nothing about your app — and that separation is enforced, not aspirational.

Ships with Populace

The engine

People with different habits — how often they post, how chatty they are, when they take a break, how fast they travel. Everyone acts at once, because sequential agents never surface a race condition.

An adapter

Answers one question per action: how does that happen in my app? The only place your endpoints, tables or auth ever appear. Anything you leave out is reported as untested rather than quietly passing.

The deliverable

A run ends with evidence, not vibes.

Every run writes a JSON report for your pipeline and a shareable page for everyone else. It exits non-zero when problems are found, so CI catches what you'd miss.

Safety

It refuses to touch production.

Populace creates real accounts and writes real rows. Aimed at production it would put invented people in front of paying customers. Three independent refusals stop that, and every one of them exits non-zero so your pipeline catches it too.

Refusal 01

The environment must declare itself non-production. Opt in, never assumed.

Refusal 02

Your production hosts are matched against every string in the target, however deeply nested. No flag overrides it.

Refusal 03

An empty denylist warns loudly — "I forgot to fill that in" is the likeliest version of this mistake.

The same principle runs through the contract: go through the front door. Use the API your app actually uses — not admin keys, not direct database writes. A simulation that bypasses your permission rules proves nothing about whether they work.

Getting started

Four commands.

populace init

Scaffolds a config and a blank adapter. You fill in where your test environment lives, and which URLs are production.

populace doctor

Checks config, reachability and coverage without running — so nobody burns a run discovering the target was down.

populace run

Brings the population to life, then writes the report. Accounts are removed automatically when it finishes.

populace clean

For a run killed halfway. Identities are deterministic, so a fresh process still finds and deletes what an earlier one created.

Where it stands

What we can and can't claim yet.

Built alongside a real app — not yet proven against one

Populace was built while shipping Buzz Buzz, Gigzen's gig-driver tracking app. Buzz Buzz's adapter is the reference implementation, and the production guard is armed against its live project.

Every number on this page comes from a demo app, not from Buzz Buzz's real backend. Populace has not yet created an account against a production-grade environment. Real backends bring rate limits, connection ceilings and pagination that no simulated adapter predicts — and we would rather say so than imply a case study we have not earned.

Proven

The engine runs against an adapter that knows nothing about any real app. Scales to 200 concurrent agents in 8 MB of memory. Zero runtime dependencies. The full test suite runs with no backend at all.

Not yet

No run against a production-grade backend. The behaviour model is still shaped around mobile workers — distance, speed, earnings. Agents are scripted, not language models.

It proves your app works.

It will never tell you whether anyone wants it. These people are generated from patterns — they won't surprise you the way a customer does, they can't tell you your onboarding is confusing, and they are least accurate for exactly the users least represented online.

Use Populace to prove your app works. Use real people to decide what to build.
