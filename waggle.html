Why Waggle

A Swiggy rider and an Uber driver are often the same person. Many riders work two or three apps in a day. Each one sees only its own slice: its own earnings, its own hours, its own map. Nobody sees the whole day, least of all the person working it.

The problem

Apps that don't talk to each other, a weekly total you can't check, and nobody to ask about the road ahead.

What Waggle adds

One place that counts your distance, shows the people you know on a map, and carries road reports from riders who just came through.

What it costs

Nothing. No ads, no trial, no subscription.

Where it stands

In closed testing on Android 7.0 and later. Not on Google Play yet, so you install it directly from this page.

Tested with Populace, our tool for simulating many users at once. Its first run found three defects that a full manual test had missed. The largest hosted run since put 36 simulated riders through 7,485 API calls with no failures. That checks correctness, not load. Read the full test record, including what wasn't tested.

What it does

Money that follows the road

Earnings grow with the distance you actually cover. Sitting in traffic doesn't count, because a phone that isn't moving earns nothing. You set your own rate, the total resets at midnight, and you can see how it was worked out.

A map with people on it

See the riders you know move in real time, and search for any street or shop. Sharing your location is off until you turn it on, and only friends you've added can see you.

The people working your streets

Flooding, surges, closures and queues, posted by the riders who just came through them. Posts, photos, reels and group chats, and you can delete a message for yourself or for everyone.

A delivery shift isn't a good network

Waggle is built for a real shift: patchy signal, a mid-range phone, and a prepaid plan.
