How we work

Built for riders. Tested like it matters.

  1. 1

    Who we build for

    Riders, drivers and couriers, often on several platforms at once, with a mid-range phone and a prepaid plan. So Waggle is small, speaks 43 languages and keeps working when the signal drops.

  2. 2

    How we test

    Bugs that need two people never show up when one person tests. So we built Populace. Its first run found three defects in our own app in three and a half minutes, one of which broke every new signup. It found two in itself, too.

  3. 3

    What we publish

    Every test run, including the ones that found problems, with its limits written down. When one of our figures turns out wrong, we correct it in public.

Global

Wherever the work is

A rider in Delhi should get the same app as a rider in London. Waggle speaks 43 languages, right-to-left included, and picks the currency from where you are.

Promises

What we stand behind

Three promises, each backed by how the software is built rather than by a policy page.

Free for riders

Waggle costs a rider nothing. No ads, no trial, no paid tier.

Why it stays free: we plan to earn from fleets and businesses, not from the people doing the driving.

Privacy the database enforces

One rider can't read another's data, even if the app asks.

How: the access rules live in the database itself, so a bug in the app can't widen them.

We publish what failed

Every test run is public, including the ones that found defects in our own work.

How: the test report is corrected with dated notes. It is never quietly rewritten.

History

So far

A short history, because it is a short history.

April 2026

Gigzen Private Limited is incorporated in Bhubaneswar, Odisha, with two directors.

July 2026

Populace becomes its own product. Its engine is rewritten to know nothing about any particular app, so any team can use it.

9 August 2026

Populace's first run against a live backend. Six simulated drivers, three and a half minutes: three defects in our app, two in Populace itself.

21 August 2026

Seven runs on a local backend, up to 300 simulated drivers at once. 3.17 million API calls, zero failures, and all 740 accounts removed afterwards.

23 August 2026

Populace Studio ships for Windows, and the engine lands on npm as @gigzen/populace.

27 August 2026

The driver app becomes Waggle, and grows from 16 languages to 43.

13 September 2026

Populace runs against a hosted backend for the first time: eight simulated people, 895 API calls, no failures.
