Our mission

Build it for the people who keep cities moving — then prove it works.

Who we build for

Riders, drivers and couriers, most of them working across several platforms at once on a mid-range phone and a prepaid data plan. That constraint shapes every decision: a 7 MB download, sixteen languages at full parity, and an app that keeps accepting work when the signal drops.

How we prove it

We built our own testing platform, because the bugs that matter in a multi-user product cannot be found by one person. It found six in our own finished app — the last of them on the run meant to sign the work off.

What we publish

The runs that found nothing and the runs that found something, together, with the limits of both written down. The test report names the claims we had to withdraw.

Everywhere

A driver in Delhi shouldn't get a worse app than a driver in London.

Built for the connection people actually have

Buzz assumes patchy signal, prepaid data and a mid-range Android phone, because that is what the work runs on. A post or message written underground is queued on the device and sent when the connection returns.

Tested against the cities people actually work in

Populace models drivers moving through Manila, Mumbai, Delhi and Jakarta — real routes, real distances — rather than synthetic load pointed at an endpoint.

What we stand behind

Three commitments, each with a mechanism behind it.

A promise nobody can check is marketing. Each of these is held up by something in the code that fails loudly if it stops being true.

01

Free for workers, permanently

Not a trial and not a freemium tier. Buzz costs a driver nothing and always will.

The mechanism: the business model does not route through them. Revenue comes from enterprise supply, fleet APIs and workforce analytics — the shape that funded LinkedIn and Waze — so there is never a quarter in which charging drivers becomes the obvious fix.

02

Privacy the database enforces

One driver cannot read another's data even if the app asks it to.

The mechanism: row-level security in Postgres, not a check in the client. An app bug cannot widen it, because the rule is enforced below the app.

03

We publish what failed

Every test run against both products is public, including the ones that found defects in our own finished work.

The mechanism: the report records withdrawn claims by name. A number we could not reproduce is marked as not measured rather than estimated.

Where we have got to

A short history, because it is a short history.

April 2026

Gigzen Private Limited incorporated in Bhubaneswar, Odisha, India. MCA-registered, two directors.

July 2026

Populace separated into its own product. It began as a test harness inside Buzz; its engine was rewritten to hold no reference to any real app, which is what turned an internal script into something another company can pick up.

August 2026

Buzz signed and on Google Play's internal track — sixteen languages, 27 currencies, offline writes, photos moved to object storage.

9 August 2026

Populace ran against a live backend for the first time. Six simulated drivers found five bugs in Buzz in three and a half minutes.

15 August 2026

Thirty-six concurrent drivers sustained for ten minutes: 7,485 API calls across twelve methods, zero failures.
