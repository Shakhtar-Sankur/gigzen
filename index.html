Mission

Build it for the people who
keep cities moving — then prove it.

Who we build for

Riders, drivers and couriers, most of them working across several platforms at once on a mid-range phone and a prepaid data plan. That constraint shapes every decision: an 8.2 MB download, forty-three languages, and an app that keeps accepting work when the signal drops.

How we prove it

We built our own testing platform, because the bugs that matter in a multi-user product cannot be found by one person. It found six in our own finished app — the last of them on the run meant to sign the work off.

What we publish

The runs that found nothing and the runs that found something, together, with the limits of both written down. The test report names the claims we had to withdraw.

Global

Everywhere

A driver in Delhi shouldn't get a worse app than a driver in London. Waggle ships in forty-three languages — right-to-left included — and picks the currency from where you actually are.

Promises

What we stand behind

A promise nobody can check is marketing. Each of these is held up by something in the code that fails loudly if it stops being true.

01

Free for workers, permanently

Not a trial and not a freemium tier. Waggle costs a driver nothing and always will.

The mechanism: the business model does not route through them. Revenue comes from enterprise supply, fleet APIs and workforce analytics — the shape that funded LinkedIn and Waze — so there is never a quarter in which charging drivers becomes the obvious fix.

02

Privacy the database enforces

One driver cannot read another's data even if the app asks it to.

The mechanism: row-level security in Postgres, not a check in the client. An app bug cannot widen it, because the rule is enforced below the app.

03

We publish what failed

Every test run against both products is public, including the ones that found defects in our own finished work.

The mechanism: the report records withdrawn claims by name. A number we could not reproduce is marked as not measured rather than estimated.

History

Where we have got to

A short history, because it is a short history.

April 2026

Gigzen Private Limited incorporated in Bhubaneswar, Odisha, India. MCA-registered, two directors.

July 2026

Populace separated into its own product. It began as a test harness inside the driver app; its engine was rewritten to hold no reference to any real app, which is what turned an internal script into something another company can pick up.

9 August 2026

Populace ran against a live backend for the first time. Six simulated drivers found five bugs in three and a half minutes.

21 August 2026

Two hundred concurrent drivers across twenty cities: 932,455 API calls, thirteen methods, zero failures. A 300-driver run reached 1.4 million calls the same day.

23 August 2026

Populace Studio released — the engine in a window, downloadable and needing nothing else installed. Seven releases in a day, every one of them fixing something that using it exposed. The engine reached npm as @gigzen/populace 1.0.0, with no runtime dependencies.

27 August 2026

The driver app became Waggle, on the package com.gigzen.waggle. Language coverage went from sixteen to forty-three, which is every country on the launch list. Play treats the new package as a new app, so the earlier internal-testing track belonged to the old one.
